Why NVR Hardware Makers Should Choose a Software Partner

2026.04.28
NVR VMS Hardware Partnership Video Security Software B2B

Building good hardware and making that hardware sell are two entirely different things.

Korean NVR manufacturers have outstanding hardware design capabilities. Thermal design, EMI certification, supply-chain optimization — they boast world-class engineering in every one of these areas. So why do they end up in price wars in the field against products shipped with bundled Chinese software?

Without software, hardware is just a component.

The Reality of Building Software In-House

Developing your own VMS software for an NVR comes with the following costs:

  • Development time: at least 18–24 months (to complete core features)
  • Development staff: a minimum of 6 people — 3 backend, 2 frontend, 1 QA
  • Annual maintenance: 20–30% of development cost
  • Security certification (NIS Security Function Verification): an additional 6–12 months

Total initial investment: at least 500 million to 1 billion KRW. And even after that investment, there is no guarantee the result will surpass software already proven in the market.

How a Software Partnership Creates Differentiation

Hardware running NOX NVR can deliver the following right away:

1. A proven feature set from day one

  • Broad ONVIF Profile S/T/M compatibility
  • 128-channel simultaneous streaming and recording
  • AI analytics (LPR, intrusion detection, line crossing, motion detection)
  • Compliance with NIS (National Intelligence Service) Security Requirements V3.0

2. Fast customization with an API-first architecture

UI customization to match your hardware brand, features for specific vertical markets — NOX is designed around APIs, so integrating with existing hardware control systems is straightforward. Integration with external systems via WebHook is available out of the box.

3. Priority distributor negotiation rights

Hardware manufacturers that partner early can receive priority negotiation rights for distribution in their region or vertical market. It is a chance to claim the market before competitors ship the same software.

In Practice: How SI Companies Choose

Purchasing managers at large system integrators put it this way:

"Hardware specs are all pretty similar these days. What we look at is software maturity, maintenance support, and AI features. If you fail on those criteria, it doesn't matter how low your price is."

In public-sector procurement it is even clearer. Without the NIS Security Function Verification, you cannot even bid. Earning that certification with in-house software takes at least another year.

Conclusion: Focus on Your Core Competency

A hardware manufacturer's core competency is hardware. Rather than building a software team and investing five years, entering the market within six months with a proven software partner is the better strategy.

"You build the hardware. We'll supply the software."
